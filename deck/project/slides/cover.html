Fee matching: from an APM draft to a feature engineering can build

Problem

Parents and friends pay, one transfer covers two students, and screenshots stand in for bank proof. Money sits unmatched and receipts go wrong.

Solution

Automate only certain matches. Route the rest to evidence-based review, approved by a second person, posted once, receipted once.

Control principle

Verified, matched, allocated, posted and receipted are five separate states. A name or a screenshot never proves money arrived.

Yasin Jariwala · AVP – Product Management · 27 October 2026 · with a working C# prototype

Open with the one-line thesis: the draft treated "we matched a name" or "a student uploaded a screenshot" as "the fee is paid". Everything in this deck follows from separating five states. Mention up front that the logic is not only on slides: there is a working prototype and a C# application with server-side permissions, and every one of the nine sample payments runs through it. Likely question: why not just trust UPI screenshots to speed things up? Answer: a screenshot can show a failed, pending or edited payment; it is kept as evidence and linked automatically when the bank line arrives, so it speeds investigation without ever moving a balance.
