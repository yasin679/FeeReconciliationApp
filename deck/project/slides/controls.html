Financial controls and business rules

Eight rules keep every rupee counted once

Verify first

Only bank-feed or Finance-matched cash can be allocated

No duplicates

Same source and ID is linked, never counted; a different amount is a conflict

Evidence for weak matches

A name or phone match needs a recorded reason

Never over-allocate

Lines ≤ received − reversed, re-checked at approval

Two people

Proposer ≠ approver; requester ≠ corrector; cash reporter ≠ verifier

Post once

Idempotency key per proposal; one receipt per line

Nothing deleted

Reversals and corrections are linked entries; receipts adjust

Audit everything

Actor, time, reason, result; refused attempts too; hash-chained

Verified = Posted + Pending + Unallocated

6 · Fee component is never inferred; claims are capped at 5 under review per student; amounts above ₹10 crore are rejected at import

These eight rules are the answer to "how are incorrect balances and duplicate receipts prevented". A balance is fee due minus posted ledger entries only; claims, cash reports and proposals never touch it. The equation is the single control Finance can check every morning, and it is exported in the reconciliation CSV. The two-person rule is by individual user, not by role, so one Finance approver cannot approve and then quietly reverse an allocation. Likely question: what happens when two reviewers allocate the same money at once? Answer: the payment carries a version; the second edit is rejected, and approval re-checks the free amount. In a race test, 20 simultaneous attempts on one ₹25,000 payment produced exactly one posting and one receipt.
